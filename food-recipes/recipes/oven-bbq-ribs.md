# Oven BBQ Ribs 🇺🇸
*American-style pork ribs: dry-rubbed, wrapped in foil, baked low and slow, then sauced and caramelised under the broiler*

![Glazed oven-baked ribs on a cooling rack](images/oven-bbq-ribs/finished.jpg)
*Photo: [Powell Family Cooking](https://www.powellfamilycooking.com/oven-baked-ribs/)*

## The story
- **Origins:** Real American barbecue ribs are cooked slowly in a pit, and Memphis style comes in two kinds: **"dry"** ribs (a spice rub only, eaten without sauce) and **"wet"** ribs (brushed with sauce before, during and after cooking). After World War II small Memphis "joints" opened with purpose-built pits. One of the best known, Charlie Vergos' Rendezvous, started in 1948 when Vergos found a coal chute in the basement of his diner and turned it into a pit ([Wikipedia, Memphis-style barbecue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memphis-style_barbecue)).
- **How it evolved:** The home-oven recipe copies the pit's idea of low heat and a long time. The food writers I read put the rack in foil at **250–300 °F** for **2½–3½ hours**, then finish with sauce under the broiler to get the sticky glaze a pit would give ([Splendid Table](https://www.splendidtable.org/story/2026/08/05/goto-barbecue-oven-ribs), [Powell Family Cooking](https://www.powellfamilycooking.com/oven-baked-ribs/)). *This is the home cook's stand-in for a pit; none of the pages claim it is "authentic" barbecue.*
- **Fun fact:** the World Championship Barbecue Cooking Contest in Memphis, held every May, is listed in Guinness World Records as the largest pork barbecue contest in the world (Wikipedia).

## Cooks' tips 👵
*No named grandmother found. These tips come from US food writers and home-cook blogs, so none is a "grandma" quote.*
- **Pull off the thin membrane on the bone side.** It is tough and stops the rub getting in. Slide a butter knife under it and pull with a paper towel for grip. **[confirmed by all 6 recipes that mention it]**
- **Marinate overnight:** rub the ribs, wrap them and chill them **8–24 hours** (4 h at the very least). The Delicious Spoon, foodiecrush's "Magic Dust" (pitmasters Ardie Davis and Paul Kirk) and Life at Cloverhill all do this. **[confirmed by 3 sources]**
- **Double-wrap with the seam on top and the bone side down** so the packet doesn't leak (Life at Cloverhill). **[tradition]**
- **Don't dip raw-meat hands back into the rub;** sprinkle it with a spoon, and keep any spare rub in a sealed jar (Life at Cloverhill's tip). **[tradition]**
- **Pat the ribs very dry** before rubbing (Splendid Table). **[tradition]**
- **Yellow mustard as a binder:** a thin coat helps the rub stick, and "you won't taste the mustard" (Beach House Kitchen). Oil does the same job (What's Mom Cookin'). **[tradition, 2 sources]**
- **Wrap tightly, double layer if the foil is thin,** so no steam escapes (What's Mom Cookin', Powell). **[confirmed by 2 sources]**
- **Test by feel, not by clock:** wiggle a bone; it should move easily. Or push a knife between two bones: almost no resistance (Splendid Table). Spices Inc. gives **190 °F (88 °C)** inside. **[confirmed by 2 sources]**
- **Pour off the juices before you sauce** (Splendid Table, Beach House Kitchen). **[confirmed by 2 sources]**
- **Stay at the oven door when broiling:** sauce goes from caramelised to burnt in under a minute (She's Not Cookin', Powell, What's Mom Cookin'). **[confirmed by 3 sources]**
- **"No science here" for the rub:** start with equal parts salt and brown sugar, add spices, and taste it on your fingertip (Dallas Moms). **[tradition]**

**Serves** 4 · **Prep** 20 min · **Cook** 3 h 15 min · **Total** about 3 h 50 min · **Difficulty** easy

> **Before you start:** this recipe has two days. **Day before:** mix the rub, coat the ribs and chill them **8–24 hours** (steps 1–4). **Cooking day:** bake, sauce and broil (steps 5–12). You need a baking sheet with a rim, heavy-duty foil, and an oven with a working broiler. On cooking day take the ribs out of the fridge **30 minutes** before baking.

## Mise en place
**Day before** (20 min of work, then 8–24 h in the fridge)
- [ ] Clear a spot in the fridge for a rimmed baking sheet
- [ ] 2 sheets of **heavy-duty foil**, each about twice as long as the rack
- [ ] A small bowl, measuring spoons, a fork, paper towels, a butter knife

**Cooking day**
- [ ] Ribs out of the fridge **30 minutes** before baking
- [ ] Oven on **275 °F (135 °C)**, rack in the middle (a second rack close to the broiler)
- [ ] Sauce ready (bottled, or sauce B made while the ribs bake)

## Ingredients
**Ribs**
- [ ] 1 large rack pork ribs (baby back or St. Louis), about **1.4 kg / 3 lb**

**1. The marinade (day before)**
*Here "marinade" means a **dry rub on a thin mustard coat**. American oven ribs use no liquid marinade. The rub is what sits on the meat overnight.*
- [ ] **Coat:** 2 tbsp yellow mustard (or 1 tbsp neutral oil). Optional, but it helps the rub stick.
- [ ] **Dry rub** (mix all of it in one bowl; it makes about **½ cup**, and you use it all):
  - [ ] 2 tbsp packed dark brown sugar
  - [ ] 1 tbsp smoked paprika
  - [ ] 1 tbsp chili powder
  - [ ] 2 tsp kosher salt (Diamond Crystal; **1½ tsp** if table salt)
  - [ ] 2 tsp freshly ground black pepper
  - [ ] 2 tsp ground cumin
  - [ ] 1½ tsp garlic powder
  - [ ] 1½ tsp onion powder
  - [ ] 1 tsp dry mustard
  - [ ] ½ tsp cayenne (optional, for heat)
- [ ] Optional: 1 tsp liquid smoke, brushed on the ribs before the rub

**2. The sauce (cooking day, choose one):**
- [ ] **A:** about **150 ml (⅔ cup) prepared BBQ sauce**, **or**
- [ ] **B (homemade, from Splendid Table, halved):** 180 ml (¾ cup) ketchup, 60 ml (¼ cup) water, 2 tbsp rice or cider vinegar, 1½ tbsp Worcestershire sauce, 1 tbsp soy sauce, 1 tbsp dark brown sugar, 1½ tsp sriracha, ½ tsp smoked paprika, ½ tsp garlic powder, ½ tsp onion powder, ½ tsp ground ginger

**To serve:** potato salad, coleslaw or cornbread; scallions (optional).

## Steps
### Day before: marinate (20 min + 8–24 h)
1. [ ] **Trim:** pat the rack dry. On the bone side, lift the thin membrane at one end with a butter knife, grab it with a paper towel and **pull it off in one go**. If there is a flap of tough meat at the top, cut it off.
2. [ ] **Mix the rub:** put the **10 rub ingredients** (sugar, paprika, chili, salt, pepper, cumin, garlic, onion, dry mustard, cayenne) in a small bowl and stir with a fork until it is **one even reddish-brown colour with no sugar lumps**. Taste a pinch on a fingertip: it should be salty, sweet and a little spicy (Dallas Moms).
3. [ ] **Coat the ribs:** lay the rack on a sheet of foil. Brush **2 tbsp mustard** very thinly over both sides (a film you can see the meat through). Then sprinkle the **rub** all over, **top, bottom and the ends**, and press it in with your hands until the whole rack has a visible coat. **Use all of it** (about ½ cup). *Sprinkle with a clean spoon; don't dip raw-meat hands back into the bowl.*
4. [ ] **Wrap and chill:** fold the foil over the rack and seal it, with the seam on top and the **bone side down**. Wrap a second sheet over it the same way. Set the packet on a **rimmed baking sheet** (it may leak) and put it in the fridge.
   ⏱ **Timer: 8–24 h in the fridge** — *minimum 4 h if you are in a hurry (The Delicious Spoon, foodiecrush). Baking straight away also works, with less flavour in the meat.*

### Cooking day: bake and glaze (about 3 h 45 min)
5. [ ] **Warm up and preheat:** take the packet out of the fridge **30 minutes** before and preheat the oven to **275 °F (135 °C)**.
6. [ ] **Bake low and slow:** put the packet, still wrapped, on the baking sheet in the middle of the oven.
   ⏱ **Timer: 3 h** — *at 2½ h, test: open one corner carefully, the bone wiggles freely and the meat pulls back from the bone ends.*
   While this bakes → make sauce B in step 7 if you use it.
7. [ ] **Sauce B (if used):** whisk all sauce B ingredients in a small pot, simmer on **low for 15 minutes**, set aside. *Skip if using bottled sauce.*
   ⏱ **Timer: 15 min** — *slightly thickened.*
8. [ ] **Check:** if not tender at 3 h, re-seal and bake **30 minutes more**. Thick St. Louis racks can need up to 4 h (Splendid Table). Pork is safe at **145 °F (63 °C)**; ribs only become tender near **190 °F (88 °C)**.
9. [ ] **Open carefully** (hot steam). Lift the rack off its foil, pour off the juices, and brush the **bone side** with **about half the sauce**.
10. [ ] **Broil:** set the oven to **broil**, rack close to the element, bone side up. Broil **4–5 minutes**.
    ⏱ **Timer: 4–5 min** — *bubbling, a few charred spots. Don't walk away.*
11. [ ] **Flip** the rack, brush the **meaty side** with the **rest of the sauce** (keep a spoonful for the table) and broil another **4–5 minutes**.
    ⏱ **Timer: 4–5 min** — *glossy and caramelised, edges just dark.*
12. [ ] **Rest** **10 minutes**, then cut into 4–6-rib portions and serve with extra sauce.

## Timer cheat-sheet
| Step | Time | Look for |
|---|---|---|
| 4 Marinate, fridge | 8–24 h (min 4 h) | Rub clinging, packet sealed |
| 6 Oven, foil | 3 h | Bone wiggles, meat pulls back |
| 7 Sauce B | 15 min | Slightly thickened |
| 10 Broil, bone side | 4–5 min | Bubbling, spots of char |
| 11 Broil, meaty side | 4–5 min | Glossy, caramelised |
| 12 Rest | 10 min | Juices settle |

## If it goes wrong
- **Still tough:** reseal in foil and bake another **30 min**; test again by wiggling a bone.
- **Mushy, falling to pieces:** it baked too long. Lift it gently, and next time check at 2½ h.
- **Burnt sauce:** the broiler was too close or too long. Use a lower rack and 3 minutes per side.
- **Foil leaked:** use heavy-duty foil, double-wrap, and always chill and bake on a rimmed sheet.
- **Not enough crust:** the foil steams the rub; the broiler gives the crust. Broil a minute longer, watching closely.

## Serve, store, reheat
Serve hot with sauce on the side and coleslaw, potato salad or cornbread. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and eat within 3 days. Reheat wrapped in foil at **275 °F (135 °C)** for about 20 minutes, then brush with sauce and broil 2 minutes.

**How I reconciled them (cooking):** oven temperatures were 250, 275, 275, 300, 300 and 325 °F (median about **275–300 °F**) and I chose **275 °F** (135 °C). Foil bake times ran from 1½ h at 325 °F to 3½ h at 275 °F; I use **3 h** and check at 2½ h. Broil: 2–3, 4–5 and 5 min → **4–5 min per side**. Sauce: ½–1 cup per 3 lb → **150 ml**.

## Regional variants & substitutions
- **Memphis "dry" ribs:** skip the sauce and serve with just the rub (Wikipedia describes dry ribs). Open the foil for the last 15 minutes to firm up the crust. *(My suggestion, not from a source.)*
- **Spare ribs / St. Louis cut:** meatier, about 30–60 min longer (Splendid Table).
- **Country-style ribs, braised** (foodiecrush): brown in flour, add onions and a sauce of ketchup, sherry, soy and Worcestershire, cover and bake at 325 °F for 1½–2 h.
- **Uncovered first, then sauced** (Spices Inc.): 250 °F, 2–2½ h uncovered, sauce, then 2–2½ h covered.
- **No broiler:** finish at **450 °F (230 °C)** for 5–10 minutes (Spices Inc.).

## Confidence note
Ten US recipe sources agree on the shape of the method: membrane off, dry rub, foil, low heat, sauce under the broiler. They differ on oven temperature (250–325 °F) and time (1½–5 h), so I used medians and a feel test. I did not cook it; the rub weight per rack is scaled from several recipes and is the part most worth adjusting to taste.
