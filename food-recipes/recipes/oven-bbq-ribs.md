# Oven BBQ Ribs 🇺🇸
*Sticky, smoky, tender ribs from a normal home oven. Rub them the day before, bake them slowly in foil, then glaze them under the grill.*

![Glazed oven-baked ribs on a cooling rack](images/oven-bbq-ribs/finished.jpg)
*Photo: [Powell Family Cooking](https://www.powellfamilycooking.com/oven-baked-ribs/)*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Serves** | 4 |
| **Day before** | 20 min of preparation, then 8–24 h in the fridge |
| **Cooking day** | about 4 h, with only 20 min of hands-on work |
| **Effort** | easy; requires patience |

## The plan in 30 seconds
1. **Day before:** pull off the membrane, rub the ribs, wrap them and refrigerate.
2. **Cooking day:** bake in foil at 135 °C, then brush with sauce and grill 5 min per side.
3. **You need:** a rimmed baking tray, heavy-duty foil, an oven with a grill (broiler).

## Ingredients

**Ribs**
- [ ] 1 large rack of pork ribs, about **1.4 kg** (baby back or St. Louis)

**Rub** (mix in one bowl; makes about ½ cup, all of which you will use)
- [ ] 2 tbsp dark brown sugar
- [ ] 1 tbsp smoked paprika
- [ ] 1 tbsp chili powder
- [ ] 2 tsp coarse salt
- [ ] 2 tsp black pepper
- [ ] 2 tsp ground cumin
- [ ] 1½ tsp garlic powder
- [ ] 1½ tsp onion powder
- [ ] 1 tsp mustard powder
- [ ] ½ tsp cayenne (optional, for heat)

**For wrapping and coating**
- [ ] 2 tbsp yellow mustard (a thin coat so the rub sticks; you won't taste it)
- [ ] Heavy-duty foil

**Sauce** (pick one)
- [ ] **Easy:** 150 ml bottled BBQ sauce
- [ ] **Homemade:** 180 ml ketchup, 60 ml water, 2 tbsp cider or rice vinegar, 1½ tbsp Worcestershire sauce, 1 tbsp soy sauce, 1 tbsp brown sugar, 1½ tsp sriracha, ½ tsp each of smoked paprika, garlic powder, onion powder and ground ginger

## Day before (20 min)

1. [ ] **Remove the membrane.** Turn the rack of ribs bone-side up. Slide a butter knife under the thin skin at one end, grab it with a paper towel and pull it off in one piece. It is tough and keeps the rub from reaching the meat beneath it.
2. [ ] **Mix the rub** until it is evenly mixed, with a uniform brown colour and no lumps of sugar. Taste a pinch: it should be salty, sweet and a little spicy.
3. [ ] **Coat the ribs.** Pat them dry. Brush a very thin film of mustard on both sides, then sprinkle on the rub (use a clean spoon) and press it in. Cover the top, bottom and ends. Use all of it.
4. [ ] **Wrap and chill.** Wrap in foil with the seam on top and the bone side facing down. Add a second sheet of foil on top. Put the packet on a rimmed tray in the fridge.

   🕐 Refrigerate for **8–24 hours** (4 hours at the very least).

## Cooking day (about 4 h)

5. [ ] **Preheat the oven.** Take the packet out of the fridge and preheat the oven to **135 °C (275 °F)**, with the oven rack in the middle.
6. [ ] **Bake the ribs in their sealed foil packet,** on the tray, bone-side down.

   ⏱ **Baby back: 2 h 30 min**

   ⏱ **St. Louis (meatier): 3 h**

   **The ribs are ready when** a bone wiggles freely and the meat has pulled back from the bone ends. Not tender yet? Reseal the foil packet and bake for 30 more minutes.
7. [ ] **Make the sauce** while the ribs bake (if using the homemade version): whisk all the sauce ingredients together in a pot and simmer over low heat.

   ⏱ **15 min** — until slightly thickened.
8. [ ] **Open the packet carefully** (hot steam). Pour off the juices. Move the ribs, bone-side up, onto fresh foil on the tray. Leave them uncovered and move the oven rack closer to the grill.
9. [ ] **Glaze and grill the bone-side surface.** Brush with half the sauce, then switch the oven to **grill/broil**.

   ⏱ **5 min** — the sauce should be bubbling, with a few dark spots. **Stay by the oven.** The sauce can go from sticky to burnt in under a minute.
10. [ ] **Flip, glaze and grill again.** Brush the meaty side with the rest of the sauce (keep a spoonful for the table).

    ⏱ **5 min** — the glaze should be glossy, with lightly darkened edges.
11. [ ] **Rest for 10 minutes,** then cut between the bones and serve with the spare sauce.

    ⏱ **10 min**

**Serve with** coleslaw, potato salad or cornbread.

## If it goes wrong
- **Still tough:** reseal the foil packet, bake for 30 more minutes, then test again.
- **Falling apart:** it baked too long. Next time check 30 minutes earlier.
- **Burnt sauce:** the oven rack was too close to the grill. Move it down and grill for 3 minutes per side.
- **Not much crust:** the foil steams the rub. Grill for one minute longer, watching closely.
- **Foil leaked:** use heavy-duty foil, wrap twice, and always chill and bake the ribs on a rimmed tray.

**Leftovers:** refrigerate within 2 hours and eat within 3 days. Reheat wrapped in foil at 135 °C for 20 minutes, then brush with sauce and grill for 2 minutes.

## Tips that matter
- **Don't skip removing the membrane or resting the ribs overnight.** These steps make the biggest difference, and every recipe I read recommends removing the membrane.
- **Test by feel, not just by the clock.** Ovens vary. A bone that wiggles, or a knife that slides between two bones, means the ribs are tender.
- **No grill?** Finish at 230 °C for 5–10 minutes instead.
- **Want Memphis "dry" ribs?** Skip the sauce and serve with just the rub.

## Where this comes from
Real American ribs are slow-cooked in a barbecue pit. In Memphis they come two ways: **dry** (spice rub only) or **wet** (with sauce). This is the home-oven version: the same low heat and long cooking time, with the sauce caramelised under the grill instead of over coals. None of the sources claim it is true barbecue.

I compared 10 American recipe sites; they agree on the method (membrane off, dry rub, foil, low heat, sauce under the grill) but differ in temperature (120–160 °C) and time. I chose an intermediate temperature: 135 °C. **I haven't cooked this myself.** The rub quantities are scaled from several recipes, so taste it and adjust.
