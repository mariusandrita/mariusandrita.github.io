# Sachertorte 🇦🇹
*Die Sachertorte: Vienna's chocolate cake with apricot jam, the Hotel Sacher way*

![A glossy chocolate-glazed Sachertorte with a slice taken out](images/sachertorte/finished.jpg)
*Photo: [Servus](https://www.servus.com/r/sachertorte) (Ingo Eisenhut)*

> **Source:** this is the **home recipe the Hotel Sacher publishes itself** ([German page](https://www.sacher.com/de/original-sacher-torte/rezept/), same as the [English page](https://www.sacher.com/en/original-sacher-torte/recipe/) you sent). The hotel says it is *"only an approximation of the original recipe, which of course must remain a tightly-kept secret."* I used the German page as the base and checked it against Austrian recipes.

## The story
- **Origins:** The hotel tells it like this: in 1832 Prince Metternich was expecting guests and asked for a special dessert. The cook was ill, so the 16-year-old apprentice **Franz Sacher** stepped in, and Metternich warned him on the way to the kitchen: *"Dass er mir aber keine Schand' macht, heut' Abend!"* The cake then became a regular at the imperial court ([Hotel Sacher](https://www.sacher.com/de/original-sacher-torte/rezept/)). The Wien Geschichte Wiki is more careful: it says the evidence points to the **1840s in Hungary** instead, citing a 1906 *Neues Wiener Tagblatt* article, and notes that the glaze needs chocolate-refining technology that only existed from the mid-19th century ([Wien Geschichte Wiki](https://www.geschichtewiki.wien.gv.at/Sachertorte)).
- **How it evolved:** After the Hotel Sacher ran into trouble and was sold in 1934, Eduard Sacher (Franz's son) sold the recipe to the pastry shop **Demel**. A long fight over the name "Original Sachertorte" followed, settled out of court in **1963**. Since then the hotel may call its cake the "Original Sachertorte", while Demel sells "Demel's Sachertorte". The difference: the hotel's cake has apricot jam **both in the middle and under the glaze** and a **round** seal; Demel's originally had jam only under the glaze and a **triangular** seal (Wien Geschichte Wiki; Demel has also added the middle layer since 2020, per a search summary I did not open).
- **Fun fact:** The hotel makes about **360,000** Sachertortes a year for shipping worldwide (Wien Geschichte Wiki), and says the cake's genius is its simplicity: butter, sugar, eggs, chocolate, flour, vanilla and apricot jam, "closer to Bauhaus than to Baroque".

## Pastry tips 🍰
*I found no named grandmother for this cake, so these are tips from the Hotel Sacher and Austrian recipe sites.*
- **Leave the oven door open a finger's width for the first 10–15 minutes,** then close it and bake about 50 minutes more. The cake is done when it gives slightly to a light touch. *Confirmed* ([Hotel Sacher](https://www.sacher.com/de/original-sacher-torte/rezept/), [kochrezepte.at](https://www.kochrezepte.at/sachertorte-rezept-4)).
- **Cool it upside down for a flat top:** after 20 minutes on the rack, peel off the paper, turn the cake over and let it cool completely. *Confirmed* (Hotel Sacher, kochrezepte.at).
- **Test the glaze on a wooden spoon:** it should coat it with a layer about **4 mm** thick. If too thick, add a few drops of sugar syrup (dissolve the sugar left in the pot with hot water). *Confirmed* (Hotel Sacher).
- **Don't overheat the glaze,** or it turns dull instead of glossy. Pour it **all at once** while lukewarm and spread it quickly with a palette knife. *Confirmed* (Hotel Sacher, Servus).
- **Brush very hot jam on the outside** (warmed with 2 tbsp water) and spread the inner layer about 3 mm thick. *Tradition* ([Servus](https://www.servus.com/r/sachertorte)).
- **Let the glaze set for hours:** several hours at room temperature (Hotel Sacher), 3 hours cool (Servus, kochrezepte.at), or 4–6 hours ([Gute Küche](https://www.gutekueche.at/originale-sachertorte-rezept-23354)).
- **Serve with unsweetened Schlagobers** (whipped cream), because the cake is sweet enough. *Confirmed.* Austrian recipe sites say it keeps up to a week and gets better with time (search summary; I could not tell which page).
- **Separate the eggs carefully:** not a trace of yolk may end up in the whites (Servus).

**Serves** 12 · **Prep** 30 min · **Bake** about 1 h · **Cool and set** 5 h+ · **Medium**

> **Before you start:** plan ahead, because most of the time is waiting. Take the butter and eggs out of the fridge early, and have a **24 cm springform**, a palette knife and a wire rack ready.

## Mise en place
- [ ] Soften **150 g butter** at room temperature
- [ ] Melt **130 g dark couverture** over a hot water bath, then let it cool slightly
- [ ] Separate **6 eggs** carefully (whites in a very clean bowl)
- [ ] Weigh **100 g icing sugar**, **100 g castor sugar**, **140 g flour** (and **200 g castor sugar** for the glaze)
- [ ] Line the springform base with baking paper, grease the sides, dust with flour

## Ingredients
**Cake (24 cm springform)**
- [ ] 130 g dark couverture chocolate (at least 55% cocoa)
- [ ] 1 vanilla pod
- [ ] 150 g softened butter
- [ ] 100 g icing sugar
- [ ] 6 eggs
- [ ] 100 g castor sugar
- [ ] 140 g plain wheat flour
- [ ] Butter and flour for the form

**Jam and glaze**
- [ ] 200 g apricot jam (*Marillenmarmelade*), smooth
- [ ] 200 g castor sugar
- [ ] 125 ml water
- [ ] 150 g dark couverture chocolate (at least 55% cocoa)

**To serve**
- [ ] About 250 ml unsweetened whipped cream (*Schlagobers*)

## Steps
1. [ ] **Prepare:** preheat the oven to **170 °C**. Line the base of a **24 cm** springform with baking paper, grease the sides and dust them with a little flour.
2. [ ] **Chocolate:** melt **130 g couverture** over a hot water bath and let it cool slightly.
3. [ ] **Butter cream:** slit **1 vanilla pod** lengthwise and scrape out the seeds. With a hand mixer (whisks) beat **150 g softened butter** with **100 g icing sugar** and the vanilla seeds until bubbles appear. *The German list says "melted butter", but its own step and the English page say softened butter, which I follow.*
4. [ ] **Yolks and chocolate:** separate the **6 eggs**. Whisk the **6 yolks** into the butter mixture one by one, then gradually add the **melted chocolate**.
5. [ ] **Fold:** beat the **6 egg whites** with **100 g castor sugar** until stiff and put them on top of the butter-chocolate mixture. Sift **140 g flour** over it and fold in the flour and whites gently.
6. [ ] **Bake:** pour the mixture into the springform, smooth the top and bake in the **middle** of the oven with the **door open a finger's width**.
   ⏱ **15 min** — *10–15 minutes with the door ajar.*
7. [ ] Close the oven door and bake the cake further.
   ⏱ **50 min** — *done when it gives slightly to a light touch.*
8. [ ] **Cool:** take the cake out and loosen the sides of the springform. Tip it carefully onto a rack lined with baking paper and let it cool.
   ⏱ **20 min** — *then peel off the paper, turn the cake over and leave it to cool completely on the rack.*
9. [ ] **Jam:** cut the cold cake in half horizontally. Warm **200 g apricot jam** and stir it smooth, then brush the tops of both halves with it. Put the halves together and brush the **sides** with jam as well. *Let the jam dry a little.*
10. [ ] **Glaze:** put **200 g castor sugar** into a pan with **125 ml water** and boil over high heat. Take it off the heat and cool a little. Chop **150 g couverture** coarsely, add it gradually and stir until you get a thick liquid. *Test: it should coat a wooden spoon in a layer about 4 mm thick; if it is too thick, add a few drops of sugar syrup.*
    ⏱ **5 min** — *for boiling the sugar syrup.*

    ![A pastry chef pouring the chocolate glaze and spreading it with a palette knife](images/sachertorte/step-glaze.jpg)
    *Pour it all at once and spread quickly · Photo: [Hotel Sacher](https://www.sacher.com/de/original-sacher-torte/rezept/)*

11. [ ] **Glaze the cake:** pour the **whole lukewarm glaze at once** over the top and spread it quickly with a palette knife. Leave the glaze to harden.
    ⏱ **3 h** — *at least; several hours for a firm shiny glaze (up to 4–6 h).*
12. [ ] **Serve** with a spoonful of **unsweetened whipped cream**.

    ![A slice of Sachertorte with the round seal and whipped cream, coffee behind](images/sachertorte/step-serve.jpg)
    *Served the Viennese way, with Schlagobers · Photo: [Hotel Sacher](https://www.sacher.com/de/original-sacher-torte/rezept/)*

## Timer cheat-sheet
| Step | Time | Look for |
|---|---|---|
| Bake, door ajar | 10–15 min | Cake rising |
| Bake, door closed | 50 min | Gives slightly to a touch |
| Cool on the rack | 20 min | Then turn over and cool fully |
| Sugar syrup | 5 min | Boiling on high heat |
| Glaze sets | 3 h+ | Hard, glossy |

## If it goes wrong
- **Dull glaze:** it got too hot. Keep it lukewarm when you pour it.
- **Glaze too thick:** add a few drops of sugar syrup.
- **Crumbly or dry cake:** *(typical advice)* it was probably baked too long; use the touch test after about 50 minutes.
- **Uneven top:** keep the oven door a finger's width ajar at first, then cool the cake turned over until completely cold; kochrezepte.at says this smooths the surface.
- **Egg whites won't whip:** Servus says not a bit of yolk may land in the whites; use a clean, grease-free bowl.
- **Glaze streaks:** spread it in as few strokes as possible, quickly, with a palette knife.

## Serve, store, reheat
Serve at room temperature in slices, with unsweetened whipped cream and coffee. Austrian recipe sites say it keeps up to a week and tastes better with time (search summary; I could not tell which page). Servus even chills the glazed cake for 3 hours so the glaze sets.

## Sources compared
Base: the **Hotel Sacher** home recipe ([German](https://www.sacher.com/de/original-sacher-torte/rezept/), [English](https://www.sacher.com/en/original-sacher-torte/recipe/)): 130 g chocolate, 150 g butter, 100 g icing sugar, 6 eggs, 100 g sugar, 140 g flour, 24 cm form. Cross-checks: [kochrezepte.at](https://www.kochrezepte.at/sachertorte-rezept-4) (almost identical: 140 g butter, 110 g icing sugar, 130 g chocolate, 55–60 min, rest 3 h), [Servus](https://www.servus.com/r/sachertorte) (50 min, ground almonds in the form, glaze cooked to 108 °C), [Gute Küche](https://www.gutekueche.at/originale-sachertorte-rezept-23354) (a variant with 5 eggs, 150 g of each ingredient and baking powder; 26 cm form; 4–6 h set; 2,285 ratings, 4.5) and the [Wien Geschichte Wiki](https://www.geschichtewiki.wien.gv.at/Sachertorte) (history). The amounts of chocolate, butter and flour agree within about 10% across the sources, so I kept the hotel's. Falstaff blocked fetching. None of the sources has step-by-step photos; the three photos are finished-cake pictures.

## Variants
- **With ground almonds:** butter the base paper and dust the form with ground almonds instead of flour (Servus).
- **Quick version with baking powder:** Gute Küche's variant adds baking powder and uses 5 eggs.
- **Glaze by temperature:** cook the sugar, cocoa and water to 108 °C ("starker Faden") with a sugar thermometer (Servus).
