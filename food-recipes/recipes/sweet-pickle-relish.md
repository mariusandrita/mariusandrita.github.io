# Sweet Pickle Relish 🥒
*The finely chopped, sweet-and-sour cucumber condiment that goes into burger sauce, tartar sauce and onto hot dogs*

![A jar of golden sweet pickle relish with a spoon, cucumbers beside it](images/sweet-pickle-relish/finished.jpg)
*Photo: [Savoring The Good](https://www.savoringthegood.com/sweet-relish/)*

> **Why this recipe:** our [Burger Sauce](recipe:burger-sauce) and the [Big Mac–style burger](recipe:big-mac-style-burger) need sweet pickle relish, which is hard to find in many shops. This is a small fridge batch, scaled from tested recipes. For a shelf-stable version see the canning section at the end.

## The story
- **Origins:** A relish is a cooked, pickled dish of chopped vegetables, fruit or herbs, used as a condiment. In North America the word usually means one thing: finely chopped pickled cucumber, in sweet, dill or plain versions, famously eaten on hot dogs ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relish)). The Balkans have a cousin in *kyopolou*, a relish of red peppers, eggplant and garlic.
- **How it evolved:** The sweet version is the one used in American burger sauces (see our [Burger Sauce](recipe:burger-sauce)). The tested home-canning method comes from the USDA's *Complete Guide to Home Canning* (Agriculture Information Bulletin 539, revised 2015), which the National Center for Home Food Preservation publishes ([NCHFP](https://nchfp.uga.edu/how/pickle/relishes/pickle-relish/)).
- **Fun fact:** The colour of your relish changes with the colour of the cucumbers you use, so no two batches look quite the same ([Savoring The Good](https://www.savoringthegood.com/sweet-relish/)).

## Home-preserver tips 🫙
*Relish has no grandma tradition that I could find, so these are tips from US recipe sources.*
- **Salt the vegetables, soak, then rinse and squeeze them dry.** This draws out water so the relish isn't watery. *Confirmed* ([Sustainable Cooks](https://www.sustainablecooks.com/sweet-pickle-relish/), [Savoring The Good](https://www.savoringthegood.com/sweet-relish/); NCHFP salts and soaks too).
- **Use vinegar with 5% acidity and don't cut down the vinegar.** *Confirmed* ([NCHFP](https://nchfp.uga.edu/how/pickle/relishes/pickle-relish/), search summary for the "don't reduce" part).
- **Scoop the seeds out of cucumbers that have many,** and choose sweeter peppers (red) if you can. *Author's tips* (Savoring The Good).
- **Wait 3 days before using for the best flavour.** *Confirmed* (Savoring The Good; Sustainable Cooks waits a week for canned jars).
- **Tie whole spices in a bag** so you can lift them out. *Confirmed* (NCHFP).
- **Use pickling or canning salt** (all three sources do). *Confirmed.*
- **In burger sauce use it drained,** and a splash of the relish brine can stand in for "sweet pickle juice". *My suggestion, based on the sauce recipes' juice tip.*

**Makes** about 2½ cups (600 ml) · **Prep** 20 min · **Soak** 3 h · **Cook** 15 min · **Easy**

> **Before you start:** this is a refrigerator relish, not shelf-stable. You need a large bowl, a colander and clean jars with lids.

## Mise en place
- [ ] **4 medium pickling cucumbers** (about 400 g), washed
- [ ] **½ red and ½ green bell pepper** (about 150 g)
- [ ] **½ large white onion** (about 80 g)
- [ ] **2½ tbsp (about 45 g) pickling salt**
- [ ] **1¼ cups (300 ml) apple cider vinegar**, 5% acidity, and **¾ cup (150 g) sugar**
- [ ] **1 tsp mustard seeds, ½ tsp celery seeds, ½ tsp ground turmeric**
- [ ] Optional: 3 whole cloves and 3 allspice berries in a spice bag or tea ball
- [ ] Clean jar(s) with lids

## Ingredients (about 2½ cups)
**Vegetables (4 cups finely diced)**
- [ ] 4 medium pickling cucumbers (about 400 g, 2½ cups diced)
- [ ] ½ red bell pepper and ½ green bell pepper (1 cup diced)
- [ ] ½ large white onion (½ cup diced)

**Soak**
- [ ] 2½ tbsp (about 45 g) pickling or canning salt
- [ ] Cold water and a handful of ice cubes, enough to cover

**Brine**
- [ ] 1¼ cups (300 ml) apple cider vinegar (5% acidity); white vinegar also works
- [ ] ¾ cup (150 g) sugar
- [ ] 1 tsp mustard seeds
- [ ] ½ tsp celery seeds
- [ ] ½ tsp ground turmeric
- [ ] Optional: 3 whole cloves and 3 allspice berries

## Steps
1. [ ] **Dice:** halve the **4 cucumbers** lengthwise and scoop out the seeds if there are many; seed the **peppers**. Dice the cucumbers, **½ red and ½ green pepper** and **½ onion** very fine (about 3 mm, so it suits burger sauce). You should have about **4 cups**.
2. [ ] **Salt:** put the vegetables in a large bowl, sprinkle over **2½ tbsp pickling salt** and stir. Cover with cold water and a handful of ice and leave to soak.
   ⏱ **3 h** — *the sources soak 2–4 hours.*
3. [ ] **Rinse and squeeze:** tip the vegetables into a colander, **rinse well under cool water**, drain, then squeeze out as much liquid as you can with clean hands or a cloth. *Skipping this makes a watery relish.*
4. [ ] **Brine:** in a pot, bring **1¼ cups vinegar** and **¾ cup sugar** to a boil, stirring until the sugar dissolves. Add **1 tsp mustard seeds**, **½ tsp celery seeds**, **½ tsp turmeric** (and the spice bag) and boil briefly.
   ⏱ **2 min** — *sugar dissolved, spices infusing.*
5. [ ] **Cook:** add the drained vegetables, stir well and simmer gently, stirring now and then.
   ⏱ **10 min** — *vegetables coated and slightly softened, still with bite.*
6. [ ] **Cool:** take the pot off the heat, remove the spice bag, and let the relish cool to room temperature.
   ⏱ **2 h** — *room temperature.*
7. [ ] **Jar and chill:** spoon the relish with its brine into clean jars, close them and refrigerate. For the best flavour wait **3 days** before using.
8. [ ] **Use it:** for the Burger Sauce take **2 tbsp (30 ml)** of relish, drained.

## Timer cheat-sheet
| Step | Time | Look for |
|---|---|---|
| Salt soak | 3 h | Water drawn out of the vegetables |
| Brine | 2 min | Sugar dissolved |
| Simmer | 10 min | Slightly softened, still crisp-tender |
| Cool | 2 h | Room temperature |
| Mellow in the fridge | 3 days | Flavours blended |

## If it goes wrong
- **Watery relish:** the vegetables weren't squeezed dry after rinsing. Drain it in a sieve before using.
- **Too salty:** rinse the vegetables longer next time.
- **Too sharp:** it mellows after 3 days; add a little more sugar to the next batch.
- **Dull colour:** it depends on your cucumbers and peppers (Savoring The Good); red or yellow peppers instead of green give a brighter jar.
- **Mushy:** too fine a dice or too long a simmer; keep to 10 minutes.

## Serve, store, reheat
Keep it **refrigerated** in a clean, closed jar. Savoring The Good says up to **3 months** in the fridge. A quick-pickle source in my search summary said about 2 weeks for refrigerator pickles, so the sources disagree; I'd use it within **1 month** *(typical advice)* and throw it out at the first sign of mould, an off smell or slimy pieces. Use it in burger sauce, tartar sauce, potato or egg salad, or on hot dogs.

## Variants
**Shelf-stable (canned) relish:** do **not** scale this down. Use the National Center for Home Food Preservation's tested recipe as written ([NCHFP](https://nchfp.uga.edu/how/pickle/relishes/pickle-relish/)). It makes about 9 pints: 3 qt chopped cucumbers, 3 cups each of chopped sweet green and red peppers, 1 cup chopped onions, ¾ cup canning or pickling salt, 4 cups ice, 8 cups water, 2 cups sugar, 4 tsp each of mustard seed, turmeric, whole allspice and whole cloves, and 6 cups white vinegar (5%). Soak the vegetables in the salted ice water for 4 hours, drain, re-cover with fresh ice water for 1 hour, drain again. Bring the spices (in a bag), sugar and vinegar to a boil and pour over the vegetables, cover and refrigerate for 24 hours. Then bring it to a boil, fill hot into clean jars leaving ½ inch headspace, and process hot half-pints or pints in a boiling-water canner for **10 minutes (0–1,000 ft)**, **15 minutes (1,001–6,000 ft)** or **20 minutes (above 6,000 ft)**. Read NCHFP's guide to boiling-water canners first.

*Canned relish · Photo: [Sustainable Cooks](https://www.sustainablecooks.com/sweet-pickle-relish/)*

- **Spicy:** add a diced jalapeño or two (a search-summary suggestion).
- **Sharper:** use white vinegar instead of cider vinegar (NCHFP and Sustainable Cooks do).
- **No time:** finely chop sweet gherkins or sweet pickles and use them in place of relish (Taste of Home does this in its [burger sauce](recipe:burger-sauce)).
