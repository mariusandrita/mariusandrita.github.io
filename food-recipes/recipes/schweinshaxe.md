# Schweinshaxe / Stelze 🇦🇹
*Austrian pork knuckle: simmered gently, then roasted in dark beer until the rind crackles*

![Crispy Schweinshaxe with cabbage salad and pickled peppers](images/schweinshaxe/finished.jpg)
*Photo: [Servus](https://www.servus.com/r/schweinshaxe-mit-warmem-krautsalat)*

## The story
- **Origins:** *Haxe* (also *Hachse*, *Hax'n*) is an old word: Middle High German *hahsen* meant the hock of a horse or dog, and the Old French word *hox(e)ne* ("calf, lower leg") may be its origin. In written Austrian German, the cut is called ***Stelze*** ([Wien Geschichte Wiki](https://www.geschichtewiki.wien.gv.at/Haxe%28n%29), [ichkoche](https://www.ichkoche.at/-lexikon/h/haxn-hachse-oder-stelze)).
- **How it evolved:** In Vienna the whole roasted pork knuckle, served hot or cold, was a favourite *"Unterlage zum Wein"* (something solid to go with wine), sold ready to eat by butchers and at the *Heuriger* (wine tavern). Veal knuckle was the version cooked at home as a main meal (Wien Geschichte Wiki). Today the crispy *Grillstelze* is the best-loved version (ichkoche).
- **Fun fact:** The same cut has different names in each country (*Haxn*, *Stelze*, and Swiss *Wädli*), and Servus says a proper Haxe comes with crisp farmhouse bread, fresh pretzels or *Erdäpfelknödel* (potato dumplings).

*The sources I could read give no date or inventor for the dish itself, so I don't invent one.*

## Austrian cooks' tips 👵
*I found no named grandmother for this dish. These tips come from Austrian magazines and recipe sites.*
- **The water must not boil hard.** Keep it just under a simmer. *"Das Wasser soll nicht mehr kochen!"* (Gute Küche); "moderate Temperatur" (Servus). **[confirmed by 2 sources]**
- **Cut the rind in diamonds, never into the meat,** with a very sharp knife (kochrezepte.at, Servus). **[confirmed by 2 sources]**
- **Score the rind while the knuckle is still warm from the pot:** it cuts more easily (Servus, Schweinshaxe mit warmem Krautsalat). **[tradition]**
- **Baste with the pan juices every 15 minutes** during the long roast (Servus, Knusprige Schweinshaxn; Servus Krautsalat version "mehrmals"). **[confirmed by 2 sources]**
- **Salt the cooked rind after simmering** (Servus, Biersaftl). **[tradition]**
- **Dark beer, not light,** for colour and a malty flavour in the sauce (Servus, kochrezepte.at). **[tradition]**
- **Serve leftover cold knuckle with mustard and a glass of wine,** in the old Viennese style (Wien Geschichte Wiki). **[tradition]**

**Serves** 4 · **Prep** 20 min · **Cook** 2 h 50 min · **Total** about 3 h 20 min · **Difficulty** medium

> **Before you start:** buy **fresh, uncured** knuckles (not *gepökelt*; see "If it goes wrong"). You need a pot big enough to hold them fully submerged in water and a deep roasting dish. Take the meat out of the fridge 30 minutes before you start.

## Mise en place
- [ ] Pot with **4 litres of water** on the stove (don't heat it yet)
- [ ] Peel and halve **2 onions** and **4 garlic cloves**
- [ ] Very sharp knife for scoring the rind
- [ ] Oven rack in the **lower third**

## Ingredients
**Knuckles and simmering broth**
- [ ] 2 fresh pork knuckles (*Schweinshaxn*, hind legs), about **1.2–1.5 kg each**
- [ ] 4 litres of water
- [ ] 1 tbsp salt for the water + **about 1 tsp more** to rub on the rind
- [ ] 2 onions, halved (skin on is fine)
- [ ] 4 garlic cloves, halved
- [ ] 4 bay leaves
- [ ] 2 tsp caraway seeds (*Kümmel*)
- [ ] 2 tsp black peppercorns
- [ ] Optional (one source each): 6 allspice berries, 1 tsp juniper berries, 1 clove

**Roasting and sauce**
- [ ] 250 ml dark beer (*dunkles Bier*)
- [ ] 250 ml of the simmering broth
- [ ] 1 tsp sugar
- [ ] 1 tsp cornstarch (*Speisestärke*) + 2 tbsp cold water
- [ ] Salt and pepper

**Warm cabbage salad (*warmer Krautsalat*, Servus)**
- [ ] 1 small head of early cabbage (*Frühkraut*), about 700 g *(typical; Servus gives no weight)*
- [ ] 80 g onion
- [ ] 4 tbsp sunflower oil
- [ ] 150 ml clear vegetable (or meat) broth
- [ ] 1 tsp caraway seeds
- [ ] 50 ml apple cider or wine vinegar
- [ ] 1 tbsp sugar
- [ ] Salt and pepper

**To serve:** mustard, grated horseradish (*Kren*), pickled peppers (*Pfefferoni*), farmhouse bread, pretzels or potato dumplings.

## Steps
1. [ ] **Simmer:** bring **4 litres of water** with **1 tbsp salt**, **2 onions**, **4 garlic cloves**, **4 bay leaves**, **2 tsp caraway** and **2 tsp peppercorns** to the boil. Lower the heat, add the **2 knuckles** and keep the water *barely trembling*, never boiling.
   ⏱ **Timer: 90 min** — *meat gives when you press it but is not yet falling off the bone.*
   While the knuckles simmer, prepare the cabbage salad in step 9.
2. [ ] **Lift out and dry:** take the knuckles out and pat them dry with kitchen paper. **Save 250 ml of the broth** (plus the onions and garlic). While the rind is still warm, score it in a **diamond pattern**, with cuts about 1 cm apart, *rind only, not the meat.* Rub **about 1 tsp of salt** and a little pepper onto the rind.
3. [ ] **Preheat** the oven to **180 °C fan** (about 200 °C top/bottom).
4. [ ] **Set up the dish:** stand the knuckles upright in a deep roasting dish. Pour in **250 ml dark beer** and **250 ml of the broth**, and scatter the **simmered onions and garlic** around them.
5. [ ] **Roast** on the lower rack, spooning the juices over the rind **every 15 minutes**.
   ⏱ **Timer: 60 min** — *rind starting to blister, juices dark and reduced.*
6. [ ] **Crisp:** turn the oven up to **220 °C** and roast **without basting any more**. Watch closely from the 10-minute mark onwards.
   ⏱ **Timer: 15–20 min** — *rind puffed, golden brown and crackling when tapped.*
7. [ ] **Rest** the knuckles on a board, loosely tented, for **10 minutes**. The meat should come off the bone with a fork. Pork is safe at **63 °C**, but this cut is only tender far above that.
8. [ ] **Sauce:** strain the roasting juices into a pot. Melt **1 tsp sugar** in a dry pan until light brown (a few seconds), add the juices and simmer until reduced by a third. Stir **1 tsp cornstarch** into **2 tbsp cold water**, add the mixture to the sauce and boil for **1 minute**. Season with salt and pepper.
   ⏱ **Timer: 10 min** — *sauce coats a spoon.*
9. [ ] **Cabbage salad** (during the simmer): shred **700 g cabbage** very finely. Soften **80 g sliced onion** in **4 tbsp oil**, add **150 ml broth** and **1 tsp caraway**, and simmer **5 minutes**. Stir in **50 ml vinegar**, **1 tbsp sugar**, salt and pepper, add the cabbage, bring to the boil **once** and take it off the heat. *It should stay slightly crunchy.*
10. [ ] **Serve** the knuckles with the sauce, warm cabbage salad, mustard, horseradish and pickled peppers.

## Timer cheat-sheet
| Step | Time | Look for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Simmer | 90 min | Water barely trembling; meat yields when pressed |
| 5 Roast, basting | 60 min | Dark juices, rind blistering |
| 6 Crisp | 15–20 min | Puffed, golden, crackling |
| 7 Rest | 10 min | Meat releases from the bone |
| 8 Sauce | 10 min | Coats a spoon |

## If it goes wrong
- **Rind stays leathery:** it was damp or the oven was too cool. Pat the rind dry and raise the oven temperature to **230 °C** for 10 more minutes, or grill the top for 3–5 minutes (watch it).
- **Meat is tough:** the water boiled hard, or it needs longer. Return it to a **180 °C** oven with some broth for 30 minutes.
- **Too salty:** you used a *gepökelte* (cured) knuckle. Cured Stelze needs a shorter boil and no salt in the water (Servus boils it only 30 min).
- **Sauce is thin:** reduce further or add a little more cornstarch slurry.

## Serve, store, reheat
Serve at once with the sauce, warm cabbage salad, mustard and horseradish, plus bread, pretzels or *Erdäpfelknödel*. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and eat within 3 days. Reheat uncovered at **180 °C** for 20 minutes to bring the crackling back. Cold knuckle served with mustard, pickles and a glass of wine is traditional at the Heuriger.

**How I reconciled them:** three of the five recipes simmer first (30, 90 and 150 min → median **90 min**). Oven: low phase 160–180 °C, finish 200–220 °C → **180 °C, then 220 °C**. Beer: 150 / 250 / 250 / 300 ml → **250 ml**. Quantity: Servus uses 1–1.5 kg per knuckle and Gute Küche 1.5 kg for 4, so **two knuckles for 4 people** makes a generous portion, though much of the weight is bone. Salt in the simmering water ranged from 2 tsp in 4 L to 2–3 tsp in 2 L, so I use **1 tbsp in 4 L** plus salt on the rind. The ichkoche *Bayrische Bierhaxe* page loaded without a readable recipe, so it is not used.

## Regional variants & substitutions
- **No pre-boil, straight into the oven** (Servus Knusprige, kochrezepte.at): a longer roast (about 2.5–3 h) and slightly drier meat.
- **Beer at the end:** Servus Knusprige bastes with dark beer in the last 30 minutes; I add the beer at the start and stop basting during the final stage for drier crackling.
- **Cured (*gepökelte*) Stelze** (Servus Krautsalat version): simmer only 30 minutes, then roast.
- **Veal knuckle (*Kalbshaxe*):** the usual home-cooking version in Vienna (Wien Geschichte Wiki); more delicate.
- **Beer-free:** use broth instead (Gute Küche has no beer).

## Confidence note
The five Austrian recipes agree on scored rind, caraway, garlic, dark beer and a hot finish. They disagree on the pre-boil (none to 150 min) and the oven temperatures, so the times above are medians. The weight of the cabbage head is my estimate; Servus gives none.
